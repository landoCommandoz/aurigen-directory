===== Rules (946 chars) =====
Before you start
DETAILING
Kit 01 directions
Page 3
Before you start
Six rules. Break one and you could be paying for a repaint.
Shade, not sun
Sun heats the panel and dries product before it can work.
No wind
Wind drops dust under the pad. Dust under a spinning pad is sandpaper.
Test spot first
Every car. The paint picks the combo, not your guess.
Stay off edges
Clear coat is thinnest on edges and body lines. Tape them.
Nail catches? It stays.
Show the customer before you start. Never promise it.
Dropped clay? Toss it.
It picked up grit. Cut bars into pieces so a drop costs one piece.
Detailing a motorcycle? Five changes
Hand-polish tanks and fenders. The 6 inch pad is too big.
Denim (matte) paint: no compound, polish, or wax. Matte-safe cleaner only.
Windshields are plastic: no alcohol, glass cleaner, compound, or clay.
Chrome gets chrome polish, then a coat of ceramic wax.
Nothing slick on brakes, tires, grips, pegs, or the seat.

Steps 1 and 2: wash and clay
DETAILING
Part A: Polish
Page 4
1
Wash and dry
Top down, then dry every gap
Rinse, then wash top to bottom with a clean mitt.
Rinse again and dry completely: mirrors, handles, badges, and panel gaps.
Water left in the gaps runs out later and streaks your work.
Let the panels cool before any machine touches them.
2
Clay
Pull out what washing leaves behind
Bag test: put your hand in a sandwich bag and glide it over the paint. Gritty means clay it.
Soak a 2 x 2 ft section with lube. Glide the clay in straight lines with light pressure.
Smooth and quiet? Wipe the section and move on.
Fold the clay to a clean face when it looks dirty.

Part A: Polish

3
Inspect and measure
Know the paint before you cut it
Zero the gauge on its plate. Read a door jamb first: that is your thin number.
Take 3 to 5 readings per panel and write them on the job sheet, page 11.
Way thicker than the rest means repaint. Way thinner: go light or skip it.
Walk the car with the light at an angle. Photograph every flaw you find.
4
Tape it off
Protect what polish can't fix
Tape rubber trim, plastic trim, emblems, and sharp body lines.
Keep the pad a finger's width off every taped edge.
Cheap beige tape can leave residue once it heats up. Test yours on the scrap hood first.

===== Step5 (783 chars) =====
Step 5: test spot
DETAILING
Part A: Polish
Page 6
5
Test spot
Mildest combo first. Let the paint decide.
Tape a 2 x 2 ft square on the worst panel. Split it in half with tape so you can compare.
Half 1: polish (E) on a finishing pad (C). Wipe with the alcohol spray (K) and check with the light (I).
Swirls gone?
Yes
Do the whole car with this combo. It's a one-step.
No
Half 2: compound (D) on microfiber (B), then polish (E) on foam (C). Wipe and check again.
Gone now?
Yes
Stop. Show the customer both halves. Re-quote as a two-step before you touch the rest.
No
Too deep to polish out. It stays. Note it on the job sheet and don't chase it.
Letters match page 2. B cutting pad, C finishing pad, D compound, E polish, I flashlight, K alcohol spray. Machine technique is on page 7.

===== Steps67 (901 chars) =====
Steps 6 and 7: polish and check
DETAILING
Part A: Polish
Page 7
6
Polish
Flat, slow, and let the machine do the work
4 pea-size drops on the pad. Spread on a low speed, then work on speed 4 to 6.
Keep the pad flat. Draw a marker line on it. If the line stops spinning, ease up.
About 10 lb of pressure. Practice on a bathroom scale.
Slow passes, left to right, then up and down. 4 to 6 passes per section.
Too hot to keep your hand on? Stop and let it cool.
Clean the pad after every section. Swap it when it's loaded or hot.
7
Wipe and check
Polish oils hide swirls. Alcohol shows the truth.
Mist the section with the 50/50 alcohol spray. Wipe with a clean towel.
Check with the light at an angle, then again from a few feet back.
Paint color on the pad? Stop. You're through the clear. Old single-stage paint is the only exception.
Still swirled: one more set of passes. Still there: back to step 5.

8
Protect
Same day. Freshly polished paint is bare.
Shade and cool paint. Thin coat, one panel at a time, by hand or on speed 2 to 3 with a clean finishing pad.

Interior rules
Spray the towel, not the panel. Overspray lands on glass and screens.
Nothing shiny on the dash. It glares in the windshield and grabs dust.
Screens get a dry microfiber or a screen-safe cleaner. Nothing else.
Headliner: barely damp, blot only. Soak it and the glue lets go.
Pet hair: rubber brush or pumice stone first, then vacuum.
Wet carpet needs air before handoff. Crack the windows or run the fan.

===== Mobile (1748 chars) =====
Working mobile
DETAILING
Working mobile
Page 15
Their driveway, your shop
Settle water, power, and parking on the phone. Not when you pull up.
Water
Plan 25 to 35 gallons per full wash off a pressure washer. A garden hose doubles it.
No spigot: rinseless wash. Two gallons, one bucket, a stack of towels, one clean face per panel.
Rinseless is for dust, not mud. Caked mud means you rinse it or you scratch it.
Ask before you book: spigot, hose reach, and who pays for the water.
Power
The polisher pulls about 7.5 amps. Any normal household outlet handles it.
12 gauge cord, 50 ft max. A thin cord starves the motor and cooks it.
Generator: 2000 watt inverter minimum. Never run the vacuum and the polisher together on it.
Apartment job: ask which outlet is theirs before you plug into anything.
Parking and runoff
Flat, shaded, out of the traffic path, away from sprinklers.
HOA and apartment rules get settled when you book, not when you arrive.
Keep wash water out of storm drains. Gravel, grass, or a containment mat.
Runoff rules are local. Call your city once, write the answer down, stop guessing.
Weather and the calendar
No wind, no direct sun, panel cool enough to hold your hand on.
Below about 50 F, polish and ceramic wax stop behaving. Garage it or move the date.
Utah winter: sell interiors and maintenance washes, not corrections.
One full detail a day. Three wash and seal jobs a day if they are within 20 minutes of each other.

What kills the buff
Eight checks, two minutes, before a price comes out of your mouth.
Check
How to tell
What it does to the job
Vinyl wrap
Seams at the edges, no clear coat shine inside the jamb
No compound, no polish. Wrap-safe wash and sealant only.
Paint protection film
Clear edges on hood, fenders and mirrors, seen at an angle
Film-safe polish, low speed, no compound. Never over an edge.
Ceramic coating
Water beads tight, the paint feels slick and squeaky
Polishing removes it. Say so before you start or you bought it.
Matte or satin
No reflection, flat finish, Denim badging on a Harley
No compound, polish, or wax. Matte-safe cleaner only.
Repaint or thin panel
Gauge reads way off the rest of the car (page 5)
Go light or skip that panel. Log the reading and tell them.
Single-stage paint
Color transfers onto the pad
Normal on old paint. Say it out loud, note it, back off pressure.
Glaze or filler job
Swirls that came back a week after a cheap detail
Strip wash first, or you polish over somebody else's lie.
Clear coat failure
Peeling, flaking, cloudy patches, rough edges
Do not polish. It spreads and then it is your fault.
Walk away from
Clear coat that is already failing.
Fresh paint under 30 days. It is still curing.
A wrapped car whose owner wants it buffed.
Flood cars, mold, or anything biohazard.
Any car worth more than your insurance covers.
When you break something
Stop. Photograph it where it sits.
Tell them before you leave. Never let them find it.
Offer the fix in the same sentence you report it.
Keep a trim clip assortment and a plastic pry set in the van.
A twenty dollar clip and an apology beats a chargeback.
Run these checks at the walkaround with the customer standing next to you, not after the wash. Every one of them changes the price, the products, or whether you take the car at all.
