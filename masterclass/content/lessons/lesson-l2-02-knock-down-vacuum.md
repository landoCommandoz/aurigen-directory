**Goal:** Knock dust down from the top, then vacuum top down with the seats slid forward and back, so nothing falls on clean carpet.

## Why you knock it down first

Dust hides high: in the headliner, in the vents, in the seams, and along the seat rails. If you vacuum the carpet and then work on the vents, everything you shake loose lands on the carpet you just did. Knocking it down first puts all the loose dirt where the vacuum will meet it once.

The knowledge base says to knock it down from the top. It does not name the tool for it yet, so that line carries an unverified badge until Lando names one.

## Why the bristle pad goes before the vacuum

Cup holders and seams pack crumbs in tight. The Dremel Versa bristle pad loosens them, and then the vacuum picks them up. Vacuum first and you leave the packed layer behind.

## Why the seats slide forward and back

With a seat in one spot, the vacuum cannot reach the whole floor under it. Slide the seats forward and vacuum. Slide them back and vacuum again. That way you reach the floor at both ends of the rail.

## Why top down again

Seats, then under the seats, then the carpet, then the trunk. Each one only drops dirt onto a surface you have not done yet. It is the same rule as the whole interior.

## Working the dwell

In a full detail, the vacuum takes 25 min, and it starts during the iron and clay dwell outside. That is the difference between running a full detail straight through and working the dwell. Lesson 6 of this level puts numbers on it.
