**Goal:** Run the 8 checks before any quote and know the five cars you walk away from.

## Why you check before you quote

A price is a promise. Some cars cannot take the job you are about to promise: the paint is film, or wrap, or matte, or already failing. The 8 checks find those cars before a number comes out of your mouth, so you never quote work you cannot do.

## The 8 checks, and what each does to the job

1. Vinyl wrap. Seams at the edges, and no clear shine inside the jamb. No compound, no polish. Wrap-safe wash and sealant only.
2. Paint protection film. Clear edges on the hood, fenders, and mirrors, seen at an angle. Film-safe polish, low speed, no compound, and never over an edge.
3. Ceramic coating. Tight beading, and the paint feels slick and squeaky. Polishing removes it. Say so first.
4. Matte or satin. No reflection, a flat finish. No compound, polish, or wax. Matte-safe cleaner only. On a bike, that includes Harley Denim paint.
5. Repaint or thin panel. The gauge reads way off the rest of the car. Go light or skip. Log it and tell the customer.
6. Single-stage paint. Color transfers to the pad. That is normal on old paint. Say it, note it, and back off pressure. Rule 10 still stands: paint color on the pad means stop, and old single-stage paint is the only exception.
7. Glaze or filler job. Swirls came back a week after a cheap detail. Strip wash first.
8. Clear coat failure. Peeling, flaking, or cloudy patches. Do not polish.

The kit does not have a wrap-safe, film-safe, or matte-safe product named yet. That row carries an unverified badge.

## The walk-away list

Five cars you turn down:

1. Failing clear coat.
2. Fresh paint under 30 days.
3. A wrapped car whose owner wants it buffed.
4. Flood, mold, or biohazard cars.
5. Any car worth more than your insurance covers.

The fifth line carries an unverified badge. Lando's insurance status for customer vehicles is not confirmed, so the line stays internal until it is.

One more thing about insurance. Garagekeepers coverage is the kind that covers customer cars in your care, but one insurer says it does not pay when you damage a car through your own work on it. Going through the clear coat with a polisher may not be covered. Nobody should assume polishing damage is insured until the agent answers in writing.

## Why walking away is part of the job

A car you turn down costs you one job. A car you damage costs you the job, the repair, and the review. Every car on the walk-away list is a car where the risk is bigger than the price.
