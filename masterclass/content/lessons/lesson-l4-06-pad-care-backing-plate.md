**Goal:** Keep pads clean during the job, wash them the same night, and check or replace the backing plate with one that fits the 56367.

## Why a clean pad matters

A pad loads up as it works. A loaded pad stops cutting and starts smearing. So after every square, blow it out with the compressor or brush it. While you are looking at the face, check for paint color. Paint color on the pad means stop.

Swap the pad when it is loaded or hot, roughly every two panels. At minimum, a fresh yellow goes on at the halfway point of the car. A one-step uses 2 to 3 finishing or polishing pads per car. Lando has Uro-Tec yellow x3 and maroon x2, plus one black pad that is for wax and finishing only.

One pad per liquid. A pad that touched compound never touches polish.

## Why the same night

Pads get washed the same night, before polish dries in. Dried compound in a pad is a dead pad.

Pull the pad off the backing plate. Wash it in warm water with a drop of laundry detergent. Not hot: hot water is hard on foam. Work the face until the water runs clear. Press the water out, never wring it. Dry it face down on a rack, and let it dry fully before the next use. Then wipe the polish off the backing plate.

Towels are different. Product towels get washed hot. Pads stay warm. The knowledge base settled that on Oct 7.

## The HF pads, and what is still open

Harbor Freight's manual says its own pads may be machine-washed in cold water with detergent and machine-dried at medium heat. That differs from the house hand wash. It applies only to the HF finishing pads, and which way those pads get washed is Lando's call, still pending. Buff and Shine's care guidance for the Uro-Tec pads has not been checked.

## The backing plate

The backing plate is the 6 in. hook and loop disc the pad sticks to. On the 56367 it bolts on through a center hole with the Spindle Bolt. The thread is M8 x 1.25. That number comes from the HF product page, not the manual.

A loose or worn plate is one of the causes of heavy vibration, and a loose plate is one of the causes of jerking. Worn hooks on the plate or the pad, or heat, make a pad fly off or refuse to stick. On Oct 7 the polisher jerked left and right, and the notes from that night say it needs a new backing plate.

Check it unplugged. The center bolt is tight. The pad is centered. The hooks are not bald or melted. The plate is not cracked or warped: spin it by hand and watch the edge.

## The trap on the shelf

Harbor Freight sells a $6.99 BAUER 6 in. backing pad, item 64913. It does not fit the 56367. It is a 5/16 in.-24 thread, an inch thread. The 56367 is metric, M8 x 1.25. Same brand, same size, wrong thread.

## What to replace it with

Call the HF tech and parts line first: 1-888-866-5797. It is printed in the manual. Give them UPC 193175448170 and ask for the replacement backing pad for item 56367. Whether they have one is not confirmed; the manual warns some parts are not sold separately.

If they do not, the replacement is a 6 in. (150 mm) plate made for a long-throw, mounted with an M8 bolt. One backup is the ShineMate Heavy Duty plate, M8 thread with the M8 bolt included. Its fit and its 6 in. price are not confirmed. One 56367 owner reported swapping in a Maxshine plate, and another said a Griot's BOSS 21 plate looks nearly identical. Those are reports, not checks. Compare the new plate's hub to the old one before you bolt it on.

Never go smaller to stop a vibration. Long-throw machines are counterweighted for 5 or 6 in. plates. A 3 in. plate causes violent vibration and can wear out the bearings.

## Swapping it

The manual's steps: use the hex wrench to turn the Spindle Bolt counterclockwise and take the plate off. Put the new plate and the bolt on. Do not overtighten the bolt. If necessary, use the spindle lock. It is under the auxiliary handle: remove two screws and slide the handle off to reach it.

A spare 6 in. M8 plate is on Lando's purchase list.
