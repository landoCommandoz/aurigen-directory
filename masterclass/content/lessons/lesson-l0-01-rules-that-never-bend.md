**Goal:** Know the 13 house rules well enough to spot any one of them being broken in the garage, starting with the rule that the label on the bottle beats this app.

## Why there are only 13

These rules are short on purpose. Each one is here because breaking it costs a customer's paint, a customer's trust, or your money. On a busy Saturday you will not remember the reasons. You only need to see the break and stop it. The reasons are below for when you have time to read.

## Rule 12 comes first: the label beats this app

This app is built from Lando's knowledge base. The knowledge base is built from product labels, tool manuals, and Lando's own jobs. A label can change. The bottle in your hand can be a newer version than the one Lando checked. So the bottle wins, every time.

When two sources disagree, this is the order: the label on the product in your hand, then this app, then older notes and booklets, then general detailing talk. General talk from a video or a friend counts for nothing until someone checks it.

What crew does next when the label and this app disagree and Lando is not there is not set yet. Lando decides that. Until he does, the app shows it as pending with an unverified badge.

## The rules that protect the paint

Test spot first (rule 4). Every paint is different. The test spot lets the paint pick the pad and the polish, so you never guess on the whole car.

Nothing slick until the paint is polished (rule 5). No tire shine, no trim dressing, no wax before correction. It is the same reason the wash before a polish runs at strip strength: polishing follows.

Shade, cool panels, no wind (rule 6). Sun and heat dry the polish before you can finish a section. Wind blows dust under the pad. The hand test is the check: if you cannot keep your hand on the panel, it is too hot.

Stay an inch off edges and body lines, and tape them (rule 9). Edges and body lines always read low on the paint gauge. There is less there to lose.

Paint color on the pad means stop (rule 10). You are through the clear coat. The only exception is old single-stage paint, where the color is the top layer and some color on the pad is normal.

A dropped clay bar goes in the trash (rule 8). It picked up grit from the floor. You tear every bar in half and keep one piece in the case, so a drop costs one piece, not the bar.

## The rules that protect the customer and you

The walkaround and before photos happen before anything touches the car (rule 13). Damage you find after you start looks like damage you caused.

If a nail catches, it stays (rule 7). Polish will not remove a scratch that catches your fingernail. Show the customer before you start, and never promise it comes out.

No tire shine on a motorcycle, ever (rule 11). Not the sidewall, not near it. A rider depends on those tires.

## The rules that protect the business

Polish jobs happen at home, drop-off, until 25 cars are logged (rule 1). Home has shade, power, water, and the compressor. No two-step correction is sold until 25 cars either (rule 2). Only delivered jobs that touch paint count toward 25. Interior-only jobs and bikes never count.

The first five machine-polish jobs sell at a $275 founder's rate (rule 3), the same for a sedan or an SUV. Machine polishing is new to Lando. Saying "founder's rate" out loud is the honest way to price that, so you never call it a sale or a discount.
