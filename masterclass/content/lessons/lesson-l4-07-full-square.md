**Goal:** Polish one 2 x 2 ft square start to finish in about 4 minutes, wipe once, IPA it, and see no haze under the light.

## Why one square at a time

A whole car is too big to think about. A 2 x 2 ft square is not. Every panel gets taped into squares, and every square gets the same routine. When the routine is the same every time, you can tell what changed when a square comes out wrong.

This lesson puts lessons 1 to 6 together. Nothing new happens here. What is new is doing all of it, in order, on one square, in about 4 minutes.

## The routine, in plain words

Check the conditions first: shade, a cool panel, no wind. Check the panel's readings allow the machine; under 75 µm, no machine at all. Tape the trim and body lines, then tape the square.

Cord over your shoulder. Make sure the pad is primed. Put 1 to 2 drops on it. Dial at 1 to 2. Pad flat on the paint. Trigger. Spread across the whole square.

Then work it. The house works on speed 4 at about 10 lb, about 1 inch per second, in crosshatch sets: left to right overlapping by half, then top to bottom. Run 4 to 6 sets. Keep the marker line turning. Stay an inch off edges. Mist the pad if it looks dry. When the polish goes clear, it is spent. The last set floats on the machine's own weight. Trigger off with the pad still down, then lift. Set the polisher on its side on a towel.

## Speed and pressure are pending

The 56367 owner's manual says only use slower speeds for polishing, and keep pressure off of the tool so the foam pad lightly touches the surface. The house says speed 4 and about 10 lb. Harbor Freight's product page backs speeds 4 to 6 for polishing, so even Harbor Freight's own paperwork disagrees on speed. Lando decides which governs, for speed and for pressure. Until he does, the house settings stay, and this lesson shows all three lines.

## Why you wipe once

On Oct 7, Lando wiped and re-applied between passes. It cost polish and time. Now you wipe once, at the end of the square's sets, never between them.

## Why the IPA and the light

Polish oils hide swirls. A square can look perfect right after the wipe and show swirls once the oils are gone. So you mist the square with the IPA panel wipe and wipe it with a clean towel before you judge anything. The house mix is 1 cup 70% IPA and 3 cups water, which works out to 17.5% alcohol. That ratio is not confirmed yet and carries a badge.

Then check with the light held low across the paint, and again from a few feet back.

If swirls are still there, the answer is the next rung of the ladder, not more of the same. Lesson 8 covers the ladder.

## Clean the pad, then move on

Blow out or brush the pad after every square. A loaded pad stops cutting and starts smearing. Then tape the next square and run the same routine.

## Why 4 minutes matters

About 4 minutes per 2 x 2 ft square, including the wipe and the check, is the booklet's time map number for a one-step. The time map gives a mid-size sedan 39 squares on paint. It puts that at 2 h 30 m of polishing, inside a one-step day of about 4 h 40 m. Add a third for a truck or SUV, and more for hot weather, wind, or a filthy car. The first car runs long.

Track your real times. Lando prices from them.

The Job Runner shows a cutoff time and will not start polishing after it. When you are running out of time, do not start polishing after the cutoff.

## On a Tesla

Start on the hood. It is the flattest panel, so you build rhythm there. The roof is glass. Skip it. No roof, no glass.

## What this lesson opens

This is the lesson that opens the polish stage. A crew member runs machine polishing on a customer car only after Lando watches the skill and signs it off on the shop phone with his PIN. Finishing the lessons on a study phone unlocks nothing. Until the sign-off, Lando polishes, the crew member runs the stages he is cleared for, and the drills happen off customer cars.
