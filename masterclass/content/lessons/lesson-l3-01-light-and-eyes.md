**Goal:** Hold a light low across the paint to find swirls and scratches, fingernail-test each one, and photograph every flaw before work starts.

## Why the light goes low

Held low across the paint, a light shows swirls as a spiderweb. That is the first look at every car, and it is how you find what the customer will be paying you to fix.

The app names no light model. Any light held low does the job.

## Why the fingernail decides

Rule 7: nail catches, it stays. If your fingernail catches in a scratch, polish will not remove it. The fingernail test is fast, it needs no tool, and the customer can do it with you. It turns "I think it will come out" into a yes or a no.

Never promise a flaw comes out. Show the customer before you start. A scratch you pointed out first is a scratch you were honest about. A scratch they find after pickup is a complaint.

## Why every flaw gets a photo before you start

Rule 13: the customer walkaround and before photos happen before anything touches the car. Photograph every flaw you find. The photo shows what was already there. Without it, a scratch that was there at drop-off becomes a scratch you made.

## Words you will hear

- Swirls: the spiderweb under a low light.
- Holograms: rotary trails.
- RIDS: random isolated deep scratches.

Lesson 2 of this level adds the gauge. The light tells you what is on the paint. The gauge tells you how much paint there is to work with.
