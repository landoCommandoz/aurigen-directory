**Goal:** Spread slow, work the house speed in crosshatch sets at about 1 inch per second, keep the marker line turning, and stop when the polish goes clear.

## Start and stop on the paint

The pad goes flat on the paint before you pull the trigger. You let go of the trigger while the pad is still on the paint, then you lift. Starting or stopping off the paint is one of the causes of slinging. The 56367 manual agrees: start and stop the tool only while it is held firmly against the surface of the vehicle.

## Spread slow

Spread the polish across the whole square on speed 1 to 2 before you work it. Spreading at high speed is another cause of slinging.

## The work speed, and why it is pending

The house works on speed 4. Harbor Freight's product page says speeds 4 to 6 are for polishing or compounding, and 2 to 3 are for applying or removing wax or sealant.

The 56367 owner's manual says only use slower speeds for polishing, or damage may occur to the paint. It does not say which speeds count as slower. Its operating steps are written around applying and buffing wax with a foam pad and a bonnet.

Both lines are Harbor Freight's. They disagree. Lando decides which one governs. Until he does, the house setting stays, and you see all three lines everywhere speed shows up: the house line, the manual's line, and the pending decision.

One more line from the knowledge base sits on the other side. On a 20 mm long-throw, a speed that is too low is one of the causes of the polisher jerking. That line is house, and it sits next to the manual's line while Lando decides.

## Arm speed

About 1 inch per second. Slower than feels right. Your arm will want to go faster. Do not let it.

## Sets

One set is a crosshatch: left to right, overlapping each pass by half, then top to bottom. Run 4 to 6 sets on each square.

On Oct 7, Lando ran 3 passes, wiped, and re-applied a full dose every time. He used too much polish and too much time. The fix is the one you are learning: 4 to 6 sets, then wipe once at the end of the square's sets.

## The marker line

Draw a line with a marker on the edge of the pad. On this DA the pad turns on its own, so the line goes around. If the line slows down or stops, the pad has stalled. The knowledge base names four causes: too much pressure, a tilted pad, a curve, or too much product. Ease up and flatten the pad first. If it is still slow, speed up one notch and check the product amount.

The line costs nothing. Watch it on every set.

## When the polish is spent

Watch the polish change. It starts creamy, then turns see-through, then almost disappears. When it goes clear, it is spent. Stop. Working past that point is one of the causes of dusting, along with a dry pad and too much heat.

On the last set, let the machine float on its own weight. Then let go of the trigger with the pad still down, and lift.

## Never park it

Never let the machine sit spinning in one spot. Keep it moving at your arm speed until the trigger is off.
