**Goal:** Zero the TC100 only on its bare plate, take a door jamb baseline, and write down 3 to 5 flat readings per panel.

## Why the gauge comes before the machine

The light tells you what is on the paint. The gauge tells you how much paint is there. Polishing removes a thin layer of clear coat every time, so before a pad touches a panel, you need to know that panel has room to give. Lesson 3 of this level turns the numbers into what is allowed.

## Why you zero only on the plate

The TC100 comes with a bare plate. Zero it there and nowhere else. If you press ZERO on the car, it offsets every later reading. Every number you write down is then wrong, and wrong low numbers and wrong high numbers both lead to bad calls. If a reading ever looks wrong, the first question is whether it was zeroed on the plate.

## Why the door jamb comes first

The door jamb reading is your baseline. It is the number you compare the rest of the car against. Take it before any panel.

## Why 3 to 5 readings, flat, off the edges

One reading can land on a high spot or a low spot. Take 3 to 5 per panel: the middle first, then toward each corner. Hold the gauge flat. A tilted gauge gives a bad number.

Edges and body lines always read low. Keep the gauge an inch off them, the same inch you will keep the pad off them later.

Readings can swing 20 µm within a foot. That is normal, not a problem with the gauge.

## Reading the screen

- Fe means steel is under the paint. NFe means aluminum. Both read fine.
- SNG is single-reading mode.
- µm is microns. Leave the units alone. 1 mil = 25.4 µm.
- Moving dots mean it is waiting.

## Writing it down

The job sheet holds the door jamb baseline and 3 readings on each of 11 panels: hood, roof, trunk, LF fender, LF door, LR door, LR quarter, RF fender, RF door, RR door, RR quarter. LF is left front, RR is right rear.

On a Tesla, the roof is glass. Skip it. It gets washed, never measured or polished.

## Teslas read in a known range

Model Y owners commonly measure about 4 to 6 mils, which is 100 to 150 µm, highest on the hood. Lando's own hood read above 100 everywhere. Those numbers are a guide, never a reason to skip measuring.
