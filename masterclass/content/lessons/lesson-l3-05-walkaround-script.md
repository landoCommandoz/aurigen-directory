**Goal:** Run the walkaround with the customer standing there, from the bag test with their hand to the before photos, before anything touches the car. Then quote what the test spot proved, read the script word for word, and get the signature.

## Why the customer stands there

Rule 13: the customer walkaround and before photos happen before anything touches the car. The customer stands there for all of it. What they see with their own eyes, they cannot argue with later. What you saw alone, they can.

## Why the bag test uses their hand

Put the customer's hand in the sandwich bag and let them glide it over their own paint. They feel the grit themselves. Now clay is not something you are selling them. It is something they found.

## Light, gauge, the 8 checks

Walk the car with the light held low. Measure with the gauge, door jamb first. Run the 8 checks. Point out every repaint and every deep scratch. The first four lessons of this level taught each of these. Here you run them in front of the person who owns the car.

## Scratches and the fingernail

When a customer asks "can you get this scratch out?", do the fingernail test with them. If the nail catches, it stays. Read the script. Do the test spot. Never promise.

## Photos and consent

Take the before photos and photograph every flaw. Those photos are your record of what the car looked like when it arrived.

Posting is a different question. Ask at check-in whether before and after photos may be posted, plates blurred. The answer is no until they say yes.

If you find existing damage, photograph it where it sits, tell the customer now, and note it on the job sheet. If you are the one who broke something, the knowledge base is just as plain: stop, photograph it, tell them before you leave, and offer the fix in the same sentence.

## The test spot, the quote, the script

The test spot comes next, split in half. Level 4 teaches it. You quote the tier the test spot proved, not the tier you hoped for.

Then read the script out loud, word for word:

> Polishing removes a thin layer of clear coat to level out swirls. Scratches that catch a fingernail and rock chips usually can't be fully removed. I'll test one spot first and show you before I do the whole car.

Word for word matters. Every sentence in it is true, and none of it promises more than polishing can do. Your own words will drift, and the drift is always toward promising more.

The customer signs the acknowledgment: "I saw the test spot and understand what polishing can and can't fix."

## The job sheet

The job sheet holds the customer, phone, date, vehicle, color, finish (gloss or matte), package, price, the paint readings (door jamb baseline, then 3 readings on each of 11 panels), existing damage, the acknowledgment, before and after photos, the signature, and the date.

On a Tesla, the roof is glass. Skip it when you measure.

## Why this lesson is the sign-off

This lesson puts the whole level together in front of a customer. Lando signs off the inspect and measure skill when he watches you run it. Exactly what he watches for lives in the sign-off file, not here.
