**Goal:** Prime a new pad once, then use 1 to 2 drops per section and a light mist of water when it runs dry, never a full dose.

## What went wrong on Oct 7

On Oct 7, Lando ran 3 passes, wiped, and then put a full dose of polish back on the pad. He did that every time. The polish looked dry and he could not finish a section. He used too much polish and too much time.

Two fixes came out of that night. Prime a new pad once, then use only 1 to 2 drops per section. When the pad looks dry, mist it lightly with water. He asked the question straight out: wet the foam with water? Yes. Lightly misted, never soaked.

## Why you prime a new pad

A pad that was never primed is one of the causes of polish that looks dry and a section you cannot finish. So you charge a new pad first. Put 3 drops on the face and work them in with a finger until the face is damp all over. Then add 3 to 4 pea-size drops for the first square.

From then on the pad is already charged.

## Why less polish works better

After priming, each new section needs only 1 to 2 drops, not a full dose. Too much product is a cause of slinging. It is also a cause of the pad slowing or stopping, which you will see as the marker line slowing down. A full dose on every section is also one of the causes of using too much polish and too much time.

Never wipe and re-apply between passes. You wipe once, at the end of the square's sets. Lesson 4 covers the sets.

## When the pad looks dry

A pad dries out. A section can be too big, the panel can be hot, or the sun can be on it. When the pad looks or feels dry mid-panel, mist the pad face lightly with water from a spray bottle or a damp hand. Do not soak it. Re-mist every 2 or 3 sections as needed.

Keep the section at 2 x 2 ft, and keep the panel in shade and cool. Those two habits take care of two of the causes before they start.

## One pad per liquid

A pad that has touched compound never touches polish. That is why the ladder in lesson 8 keeps compound on its own maroon pad and runs the pass after it on a yellow with M210.

## What the bottle says

The polish is Meguiar's M210 Ultra Pro Finishing Polish. Meguiar's says it creates a deep, glossy finish even on dark and sensitive paints, and it is safe by hand, DA, or rotary. That is Meguiar's wording. It does not say M210 is built for dark paint.

Nobody has read the pad, speed, and priming directions on Lando's own M210 and Ultimate Compound bottles yet. Meguiar's web pages give no pad type and no speed number. A retailer's copy of the M210 label says to prime new or dry pads by misting M34 Final Inspection, a Meguiar's spray Lando does not own. If his bottle says that, it disagrees with the house water mist, and Lando decides which one you follow.

House rule 12 stands in the meantime: the label on the bottle beats this app. Read the back of the bottle before you use it. What a crew member does next, when the label and this app disagree and Lando is not there, is Lando's call. He has not made it yet, so that line shows as pending.
