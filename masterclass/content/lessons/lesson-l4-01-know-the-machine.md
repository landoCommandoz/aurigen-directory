**Goal:** Know what the Bauer 56367 is and is not, check it unplugged before every job, and run the manual's 30-second test run before it touches paint.

## What this machine is

Your polisher is the BAUER 56367 from Harbor Freight, model 1918E-B. It is a 7.5 Amp, 6 in., 20 mm long-throw random orbit DA polisher. DA means dual action. The pad orbits, and it also turns freely on its own. That is why it is called free-spinning.

A rotary is different. A rotary only spins. The Black & Decker rotary polisher in the garage never touches customer paint. It is on the never list for a reason.

On Oct 7, Lando thought this polisher was a rotary. It is not. Getting this right matters. The marker line check in lesson 4 only makes sense on a DA: the pad turns on its own, and too much pressure, a tilted pad, a curve, or too much product can stall it.

Long-throw means a big orbit. This one throws 20 mm. A long-throw moves more than a short-throw, and some float is normal. That last line is still waiting on confirmed wording, so it carries a badge.

## The dial

The dial has six speeds, about 2,000 to 4,600 OPM. OPM means orbits per minute. The electronics are constant-speed, with a soft start.

Harbor Freight's product page says speeds 2 to 3 are for applying or removing wax or sealant, and speeds 4 to 6 are for polishing or compounding. The house spreads on 1 to 2 and works on 4.

The 56367 owner's manual says something different. It says only use slower speeds for polishing, or the paint may be damaged. It never says which speeds count as slower. Both lines come from Harbor Freight, and they disagree. Lando decides which one governs. Until he does, the house setting stays, and every place in the app that shows speed shows all three lines: the house line, the manual's line, and the pending decision. Lesson 4 of this level goes deeper.

## Why you check it unplugged

An off-center pad or a loose plate is one of the causes of the polisher jerking, walking, or hopping. A loose or worn plate, an off-center pad, uneven compound, or a damaged pad is a cause of heavy vibration. On Oct 7 the polisher jerked left and right going up and down, and the fix list starts with these checks.

Unplug it first. Your fingers are near the plate. Then check four things: the center bolt is tight, the pad is centered, the hooks are not bald or melted, and the plate is not cracked or warped. Spin the plate by hand and watch the edge. Visible plate wobble is a reason to stop. Lesson 6 covers replacing a damaged plate.

The manual adds its own check before each use: loose hardware, misaligned or binding parts, and cracked or broken parts. Do not use damaged equipment.

Harbor Freight's FAQ adds two ways to reduce vibration: center the foam pad on the backing pad, and spread compound evenly around the pad.

## Why the test run

The manual has a short test called Setting and Testing. Mount the pad. Plug it in. Set the highest speed. Run it for at least 30 seconds. If you hear abnormal noise or feel abnormal vibration, have the problem fixed before you use it again. Elsewhere the manual says it even more plainly: if abnormal vibration occurs, stop use immediately.

Do the test run off the car. The highest speed is for the test, never for the first touch on paint. Turn the dial back down to 1 to 2 before the pad goes near the car.

Thirty seconds off the car costs you almost nothing. Finding the same problem on a customer's paint costs a lot more.

## Never shrink the plate

When a long-throw shakes, it is tempting to put a smaller plate on it. Do not. Long-throw machines are counterweighted for 5 or 6 in. plates. A 3 in. plate causes violent vibration and can wear out the bearings. If the plate is the problem, replace it with the right plate. The backing plate is M8 x 1.25 thread. That number comes from the HF product page, not the manual.

## When something is wrong

The tech and parts line is printed on every page of the manual: 1-888-866-5797. Give them the UPC, 193175448170, and ask for the replacement backing pad for item 56367. Whether they have one in stock is not confirmed.

## Two habits from the first minute

Run the cord over your shoulder, so it never drags on the paint or reaches the pad. When you set the polisher down, set it on its side on a towel, never on its pad.

## Bikes are different

Bikes are hand-polished. The 6 in. pad does not fit a tank, so tanks and fenders get polished by hand. A 3 in. machine comes later, bought from bike revenue.
