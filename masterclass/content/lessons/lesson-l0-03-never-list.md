**Goal:** Name every tool and product that never touches customer paint or goes in the wash bucket, and pull them before a job starts.

## Two lists, one habit

There are two lists. The first is what never touches customer paint. Those things stay in the garage. The second is what never goes in the wash bucket. The habit is the same for both: check the cart and the bucket before the job starts, not halfway through it.

## Never on customer paint

Nine things stay in the garage: the Craftsman random-orbit sander, the Black & Decker angle grinder, the Black & Decker rotary polisher, the three Turtle Wax compounds, Minwax polyurethane, Spic and Span, Armor All Original protectant, lithium grease, and dish soap.

Some of these are obvious. Nobody means to put an angle grinder on a hood. The dangerous ones are the ones that look useful.

The Black & Decker rotary looks like a polisher. A rotary only spins. Your polisher, the BAUER, is a dual action machine that spins and orbits. Lando once thought his own polisher was a rotary, so this mix-up is real. The rotary never touches customer paint.

The Turtle Wax Polishing Compound was tested and rejected. Its cap is crusted, and that crust is dried abrasive. A crumb of dried abrasive under a spinning pad scratches the paint you are trying to fix.

The rest are on the list because Lando put them there. You do not need a reason to leave something in the garage.

## Never in the wash bucket

Ultimate Wash & Wax before polishing, dish soap, Spic and Span, wheel brushes, and the clay mitt never go in the wash bucket.

Ultimate Wash & Wax is a fine soap for maintenance washes. Before a polish it leaves wax behind, and polishing follows, so that wash runs at strip strength instead. Wheel brushes carry wheel grime. The clay mitt has its own care. Neither one belongs in water that goes back onto paint.

## Nothing slick before the polish

No tire shine, no trim dressing, and no wax goes on the car until the paint is polished. That is rule 5, and it makes the never list longer on polish days.

## Bikes have their own list

No tire shine on a motorcycle, ever. Not the sidewall, not near it. Nothing slick on the brake rotors, grips, pegs, or seat. The windshield is plastic: no alcohol, no glass cleaner, no compound, no clay. Which cleaner is safe on it is not confirmed yet. The chain gets chain cleaner and chain lube only, because household degreaser strips the O-rings. Matte paint, like Harley Denim, gets no compound, no polish, and no wax.

## Teslas: the house steps and Tesla's manual disagree

Lando's car is a Tesla, and Tesla owners will be customers. Tesla's own manual says three things that run into house steps.

It says do not use chemical-based wheel cleaners or pre-wash products, because they can damage the finish on the wheels. The house wheel step uses Armor All Extreme Wheel & Tire on every car, and the house plan has a pump-sprayer pre-soak.

It says do not use cutting pastes, color restoration compounds, or polishes with harsh abrasives. Rung 3 of the house pad ladder allows Ultimate Compound when the test spot proves it is needed.

It also says damage or discoloration from cleaning products is not covered by Tesla's warranty.

Lando decides how this applies to Teslas. Until he does, none of the three is settled on a Tesla, and each one shows as pending. What is settled: the camera lenses get glass cleaner only, and the glass roof is washed, never polished.
