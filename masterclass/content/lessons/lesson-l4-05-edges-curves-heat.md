**Goal:** Tape first, stay an inch off edges and body lines, use the middle of the pad on curves, and stop when a panel is too hot to hold your hand on.

## Why edges get an inch

Edges and body lines always read low on the paint gauge. Less paint there means less room to polish. So the house rule is simple: stay an inch off edges and body lines, and tape them so the pad never wanders onto them. The same inch keeps the gauge off them in Level 3.

## Why you tape before the pad touches paint

Untaped trim and badges come out with white residue on them. On a Tesla, polish turns the satin black window trim chalky white.

Tape the rubber and plastic trim, the emblems, and the sharp body lines. Then tape the panel into 2 x 2 ft squares and work one square at a time.

Test the tape first. Cheap beige tape can leave residue once it heats up, and polishing makes heat. Try it on scrap before it goes on a customer's car.

How to clean white residue off trim, if it happens anyway, is not confirmed yet. A soft brush and a trim-safe cleaner is the line in the knowledge base, and it carries a badge until it is checked.

## The Tesla tape list

A Tesla has more to tape. The satin black window trim. The flush door handle edges. All five cameras: two on the B-pillars, two fender repeaters, and one above the rear plate. The badges. The black glass header. The textured cladding on the wheel arches and the lower bumper.

The roof is glass. It gets washed, never polished, and it is skipped in the measure and polish routes.

The house polish route on a Tesla starts on the hood, the flattest panel, so you build rhythm there. Bumpers go last, because plastic heats fast.

Other model years and other models have different cameras and trim. Look at each car.

## Curves

On a curve, use the middle of the pad. Never tilt the machine so the pad rides on its edge. A tilted pad is one of the causes of the pad slowing or stopping, and of the polisher jerking. A flat pad on a curved panel is another cause. Keep the pad as flat as the panel lets you, and watch the marker line.

## Heat

Polish in shade, on cool panels, with no wind. The panel must be cool enough to hold your hand on. That is house rule 6.

Your hand is the gauge. Keep checking the panel as you work. If it is too hot to keep your hand on, stop and let it cool. Heat is a cause of polish drying out, of dusting, and of a pad that flies off or will not stick.

Plastic bumpers heat fast. They get lighter pressure, a lower speed, and a shorter time. That is why the Tesla route leaves them for last.

Never park the machine spinning in one spot. Keep it moving until the trigger is off.

## Cold days

Cold matters too, but the number is not confirmed. The knowledge base says below about 50 F, polish stops behaving, so garage it or move the date. That line is waiting on the product labels and carries a badge.
