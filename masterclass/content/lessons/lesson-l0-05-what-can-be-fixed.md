**Goal:** Use a low light and the fingernail test to tell what polishing can fix from what stays, and never promise a customer it comes out.

## Why you never promise

Polishing levels out swirls by removing a thin layer of clear coat. It can only remove what sits in that thin layer. A scratch that catches your fingernail is deeper than polish removes. So the rule is simple: if your nail catches, it stays.

A promise you cannot keep costs more than the job is worth. The customer remembers the one scratch you said would come out, not the hundred swirls that did. So you show them what stays before you start, and you never say it comes out.

## The light and the nail

Hold a light low across the paint. Swirls look like a spiderweb. Any light works if you hold it low. The app names no model.

For each scratch, run your fingernail across it. No catch means polishing has a chance. A catch means it stays. Photograph every flaw before you start, so the record shows what was there before you touched the car.

## The test spot answers the rest

Between "clearly fine" and "clearly stays" is a lot of paint you cannot judge by eye. That is what the test spot is for. You work the mildest combo first on the worst panel, wipe, mist with IPA, and look again with the light.

Always judge after the IPA wipe. Polish oils hide swirls. The wipe takes the oils off, so you see the real paint.

If a flaw is still there after compound and polish, it is too deep. That is rung 4 of the ladder. Note it, show the customer, and do not chase it. One compound pass per section is the limit.

## Things polish does not touch

Rock chips usually can't be fully removed. Failing clear coat (peeling, flaking, cloudy patches) is never polished at all. Matte paint, wraps, and film have their own rules, and Level 3 covers them.

## Headlights are a refresh

Blue Magic by hand brightens a yellow headlight, but it does not hold. Without a sealant on top, the lights yellow again in about a month. Sell it as a refresh, $30 a pair, never as a fix.

## Say it the same way every time

The customer script says it for you: "Scratches that catch a fingernail and rock chips usually can't be fully removed. I'll test one spot first and show you before I do the whole car." Read it out loud, word for word.
