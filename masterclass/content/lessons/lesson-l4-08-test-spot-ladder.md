**Goal:** Run the test spot on the worst panel, climb the pad ladder one rung at a time, and stop at the rung the paint picks.

## Why every car gets a test spot

Rule 4: test spot on every car before polishing. The paint picks the combo, not a guess. You find out by starting at rung 1 on the paint in front of you, and climbing only when the paint tells you to.

You test on the worst panel. On Lando's Model Y that was the hood. When a combo clears the worst panel, the knowledge base says that combo does the whole car.

## Setting it up

Check the panel's gauge readings first, and find its band. The band decides how high you are allowed to climb. Compound needs 100 µm or more, and even then it is allowed, not required.

Tape a 2 x 2 ft square on the worst panel. Split it with tape so you can compare halves. You will show the customer both halves before you go further than what was sold.

## The ladder

Rung 1 is yellow + M210. Run the square the way lesson 7 taught you. Wipe, mist with IPA, wipe clean, and check with the light. Swirls gone? That is the combo for the whole car. That is a one-step. Stop climbing.

Rung 2 is maroon + M210 on the same square. The maroon has more cut than the yellow. Wipe, IPA, light.

Rung 3 is only for a panel that is still swirled and reads 100 µm or more. Maroon + Ultimate Compound, 3 to 4 sets. Wipe the compound off with its own towel. Then always follow with yellow + M210 to clear the compound haze. Every compounded square gets both passes. Never ship a compounded panel without the yellow + M210 pass.

Rung 4 is not a pad. If it is still there after compound and polish, it is too deep. Note it, show the customer, and do not chase it.

One compound pass per section, max. Polishing removes a thin layer of clear coat every time, and a scratch that survived compound and polish is not worth another layer.

## Why you IPA before you judge

Polish oils hide swirls. If you look before the IPA wipe, a rung can look like it worked when it did not. Then you polish the whole car with a combo that leaves swirls behind. Wipe, IPA, light, every rung, before you decide.

## The pads that are not on the ladder

The black pad is for wax and finishing only. It is never a rung on this ladder.

The Turtle Wax Polishing Compound was tested and rejected. It is on the never-on-paint list. Its crusted cap is dried abrasive.

## Teslas: the compound rung is not settled

Tesla's manual says do not use cutting pastes, color restoration compounds, or polishes with harsh abrasives, because they can scour the surface and permanently damage the paint. It also says damage or discoloration from cleaning products is not covered by Tesla's warranty. Rung 3 of the house ladder allows Ultimate Compound when the test spot proves it is needed.

Those two lines run into each other on every Tesla, including Lando's own. Lando decides how this applies to Teslas. Until he does, the compound rung on a Tesla is pending. It is neither allowed nor ruled out. It waits on Lando.

## Showing the customer

Show the customer both halves. If the paint needs more than what was sold, re-quote before you go further. The script you read the customer says polishing removes a thin layer of clear coat to level out swirls, that scratches that catch a fingernail and rock chips usually cannot be fully removed, and that you will test one spot first and show them before you do the whole car. The test spot is where you keep that promise.

Rule 2 sits next to this: no two-step correction is sold until 25 cars are logged.

Write the winning combo on the job sheet.
