**Goal:** Explain clear coat, base coat, single-stage paint, and microns, and why every polish removes a thin layer of clear coat.

## Paint is layers

On a modern car, the paint you see is layers. The top layer is the clear coat: clear, hard, and the only layer you polish. Under it is the color, the base coat. You can tell where one ends and the other starts, because the moment you cut through the clear, color shows on the pad.

## Polishing takes something away

Polish does not add shine on top of the paint. It removes a thin layer of clear coat to level out swirls. The swirl is a groove, and polishing lowers the clear around it until the groove is gone. That is the sentence in the customer script, and it is the one sentence to remember: "Polishing removes a thin layer of clear coat to level out swirls."

So every pass costs something. That is why you measure before you polish, run a test spot, and stop at the mildest combo that works. The paint picks the combo, not a guess.

## How much is there

Paint is measured in microns (µm). Your gauge reads microns. Some owners and forums talk in mils, and 1 mil is 25.4 µm.

Factory paint, all layers together, is usually about 100 to 180 µm. The clear coat is only about 38 to 50 µm of that. Those two figures were checked once and still need a recheck, so they carry the unverified badge. Either way, the point stands: the layer you polish is a small slice of the total.

Readings move around. A 20 µm swing within a foot is normal. Tesla Model Y owners commonly measure about 4 to 6 mils (100 to 150 µm), highest on the hood. Lando's hood read above 100 everywhere.

Edges and body lines always read low. There is less clear there to lose, which is why you stay an inch off them and tape them.

## Single-stage paint

Some old cars have single-stage paint. On those, the color is the top layer, and some color on the pad is normal. Say it out loud, note it on the job sheet, and back off pressure. That is the only exception to the stop rule. On any modern car, color on the pad means you are through the clear. Stop.

## When the clear is already gone

Peeling, flaking, or cloudy patches are clear coat failure. Polishing cannot fix it. Do not polish it. Failing clear coat is also on the walk-away list.

## Words you will hear

Swirls look like a spiderweb under a low light. Haze is the dull film compound can leave, which is why a compounded square always gets a polish pass after it. Marring is fine marks, and clay can leave it on soft or black paint. Holograms are trails a rotary leaves. RIDS are random isolated deep scratches. Etching is damage eaten into the clear. Level 3 teaches you to read all of these with a light and a gauge.
