**Goal:** Let the bag test pick the panels, then clay each 2 x 2 ft section on plenty of lube until the bar glides silent.

## What clay is for

Washing takes off what sits on the paint. Some contamination bonds to it and stays after the wash. Clay pulls that out. It is the decon step, and it comes before any polish.

## The bag test picks the panels

Put your hand in a sandwich bag and glide it over the paint. Gritty means clay that panel. Smooth means leave it. The bag test decides, not a habit of claying everything.

The hood and the roof catch the most fallout and take the longest. Plan your time around them.

## Tar first, with the right product

Bug & Tar goes on rocker tar first. If clay will not lift tar, that is a job for bug and tar remover, not more clay.

## How to clay

Tear the bar in half and keep one piece in the case. That way a drop costs one piece, not the bar. Flatten the other piece into a palm-size patty.

The lube is the clay tub mix: 1 gal water and 1 oz Citrus Wash & Gloss. Work one 2 x 2 ft section at a time. Soak the section with lube, then glide the clay in straight lines with light pressure. Never let a section go dry.

Listen and feel. Grabbing and noise means contamination is still there. Silent and smooth means the section is clean. When it glides silent, move on.

Knead the clay to a clean face after every panel, so you are not dragging the last panel's grit across the next one. Clay paint only. Skip the glass.

## Drops

A dropped bar goes in the trash. It picked up grit from the floor. That is rule 8.

A dropped clay mitt gets rinsed thoroughly before it goes near paint again. The mitt maker's own care directions are not checked yet, so that line carries the unverified badge. Never put either one back on the paint unchecked. The mitt never goes in the wash bucket.

## Clay leaves its own marks

Clay can mar soft or black paint, and clay mitts are riskier on black or soft paint. So plan a polish after clay. Light marring from clay gets polished out.
