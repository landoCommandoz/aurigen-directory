## 5. Wash and decon SOP (current: hose only, no pressure washer)

### 5.1 Order
1. Set up (section 18 kit staging). Tesla: Car Wash Mode on (section 11).
2. Wheels and tires FIRST (cleaner splashes). Wheel must be cool. Spray face and barrel, wait about a minute, brush (soft on face, long brush in barrel, stiff on tire), rinse before it dries. One wheel at a time. Wheel brushes never touch paint. `[HOUSE]`
3. Pre-rinse on jet 3 to 4 minutes, top down, extra on bumpers, rockers, and behind the wheels. Nothing touches paint before this. `[HOUSE]`
4. **Pre-soak (added Oct 7) on any car with caked dirt:** see 5.3. `[HOUSE]` method `[VERIFY]`
5. Bug & Tar foam on bugs and rocker tar: one-minute dwell, then wash it off. No scrubbing. `[HOUSE]`
6. Contact wash, roof to rockers, one panel at a time (5.2).
7. Final rinse: nozzle off, open hose, let water sheet off top down. `[HOUSE]`
8. Dry: compressor first (mirrors, handles, badges, charge port door, liftgate seams, grille, trim seams), then towels laid flat and pulled toward you. Open every door and dry the jambs. `[HOUSE]`
9. Decon: bag test, then clay (section 6).

### 5.2 Towel method (current)
- 16 microfiber towels in the bucket, one per panel, folded in quarters. `[HOUSE]`
- Lift each towel out dripping and squeeze it over the panel's top edge. Straight lines, front to back. Never circles. `[HOUSE]`
- A used towel never goes back in the bucket. `[HOUSE]`
- Upper panels may share a towel on a fresh face. Lower panels never share. Rockers and mirrors last, then retire that towel. `[HOUSE]`
- Keep the whole car wet. If a panel starts drying, rinse it. `[HOUSE]`
- Alternative (booklet): two-bucket wash with a mitt and two grit guards; rinse the mitt after every panel; dropped mitt is done for the day. New mitt: break it in with soapy water on the windshield for a minute or two. `[HOUSE]`

### 5.3 Pre-soak (new, Oct 7) `[VERIFY method and product]`
Field note: after the first wash of Lando's Model Y, a lot of caked dirt was still on the car. Without a pressure washer or foam cannon, add a dwell step:
- Pump-sprayer pre-wash on the lower third, front end, and behind the wheels. Let it dwell without drying (shade, cool panels), then rinse on jet.
- Candidate solutions: Citrus Wash & Gloss at strip strength in the pump sprayer, or a dedicated pre-wash/APC. Fact-checker must confirm the label allows it.
- First wash of a neglected car is always the worst. Expect the second wash to go faster.

### 5.4 Hard water spots (Utah) `[HOUSE]` with removal steps `[VERIFY]`
Prevention: shade, cool panels, smaller sections, never walk away from a wet car in the sun, dry immediately, compressor the gaps, use the ceramic wax as a drying aid (section 9.1), open-hose sheeting rinse.
Removal: fresh spots wipe off with a damp towel and quick detailer or the ceramic spray; mineral spots need a water-spot remover; etched spots need polish. `[VERIFY products]`
Future fix: spot-free / deionized rinse water. `[VERIFY cost]`

### 5.5 Water and runoff
- Hose uses roughly double the water of a pressure washer; a full wash off a pressure washer runs about 25 to 35 gallons. `[HOUSE from booklet]` `[VERIFY]`
- Dump buckets in a sink or toilet, not the street. Keep wash water out of storm drains. Call Tooele City once about runoff and write the answer down (pending). `[HOUSE]`

## 6. Clay `[HOUSE]`
- Bag test decides which panels get clayed: hand in a sandwich bag, glide over paint. Gritty means clay it.
- Bug & Tar on rocker tar first. If clay won't lift it, that is a job for bug and tar remover, not more clay.
- Tear the bar in half and keep one piece in the case, so a drop costs one piece.
- Flatten into a palm-size patty. Work 2 x 2 ft sections. Soak with lube (clay tub mix). Glide in straight lines with light pressure. Never let a section go dry.
- Listen and feel: grabbing and noise means contamination; silent and smooth means clean.
- Knead to a clean face after every panel.
- Dropped bar: trash. Clay mitt: rinse it thoroughly. `[VERIFY mitt maker's guidance]`
- Paint only. Skip glass with the bar.
- Clay can mar soft or black paint. Clay mitts are riskier on black/soft paint. Plan a polish after clay. `[SOURCED 2026-10-06]`
- Hood and roof catch the most fallout and take the longest.

## 7. Inspect and measure

### 7.1 Light and eyes `[HOUSE]`
- Light held low across the paint. Swirls look like a spiderweb.
- Fingernail test: if a nail catches, polish will not remove it.
- Photograph every flaw before you start.

### 7.2 R&D TC100 paint gauge `[SOURCED 2026-10-06]`
- Screen: `Fe` = steel substrate, `NFe` = aluminum (both read fine), `SNG` = single-reading mode, `µm` = microns (leave the units alone), moving dots = waiting.
- **Zero ONLY on the included bare plate. Never press ZERO on the car**; it offsets every later reading.
- Hold it flat. Door jamb first = your baseline. 3 to 5 readings per panel (middle, then toward each corner). Write them down.
- 20 µm swings within a foot are normal.
- Tesla Model Y owners commonly measure about 4 to 6 mils (100 to 150 µm), highest on the hood. Lando's hood read above 100 everywhere.

### 7.3 Thickness bands `[HOUSE]` (conservative house standard, not a manufacturer spec)
Background: factory total film is usually about 100 to 180 µm; the clear coat itself is only about 38 to 50 µm of that. `[SOURCED 2026-09-22]` `[VERIFY reconfirm]`

| Reading | What's allowed |
|---|---|
| 100 µm or more | Full ladder permitted. Over 100 means compound is *allowed*, not required |
| 90 to 99 | Polish only (yellow or maroon + M210). No compound |
| 85 to 89 | Tell the customer. Yellow + M210, light pressure, fewest passes |
| 75 to 84 | Tell the customer. Finishing polish only, minimal passes, or hand work |
| Under 75 | No machine. Hand protection only |
| Way above the rest of the car (about 220+) | Repaint. Go gentle or skip. Log it and tell the customer |
| Way below the rest | Thin panel. Go light or skip |

Edges and body lines always read low: stay an inch off.

### 7.4 What kills the buff: 8 checks before quoting `[HOUSE from booklet]`
| Check | How to tell | What it does to the job |
|---|---|---|
| Vinyl wrap | Seams at edges, no clear shine inside the jamb | No compound, no polish. Wrap-safe wash and sealant only |
| Paint protection film | Clear edges on hood, fenders, mirrors at an angle | Film-safe polish, low speed, no compound, never over an edge |
| Ceramic coating | Tight beading, slick squeaky paint | Polishing removes it. Say so first |
| Matte or satin | No reflection, flat finish | No compound, polish, or wax. Matte-safe cleaner only |
| Repaint or thin panel | Gauge way off the rest | Go light or skip. Log and tell |
| Single-stage paint | Color transfers to the pad | Normal on old paint. Say it, note it, back off pressure |
| Glaze or filler job | Swirls returned a week after a cheap detail | Strip wash first |
| Clear coat failure | Peeling, flaking, cloudy patches | Do not polish |

Walk away from: failing clear coat, fresh paint under 30 days, a wrapped car whose owner wants it buffed, flood/mold/biohazard cars, any car worth more than your insurance covers. `[HOUSE]`

## 8. Machine polishing SOP (one-step)

### 8.1 Before the pad touches paint `[HOUSE]`
- Tape: rubber and plastic trim, emblems, sharp body lines, and the Tesla list in section 11. Test cheap tape on scrap first (beige tape can leave residue when hot).
- Cord over your shoulder. Polisher rests on its side on a towel.
- Tape the panel into 2 x 2 ft squares. One square at a time.

### 8.2 Pad priming and product amount `[HOUSE]`
- New pad: 3 drops worked into the face with a finger until damp all over, then 3 to 4 pea-size drops.
- Every section after that: **1 to 2 drops, not a full dose.** The pad is already charged.
- Pad looks or feels dry mid-panel: **mist the pad face lightly with water** (spray bottle or a damp hand). Do not soak it. Re-mist every 2 or 3 sections as needed. (Oct 7 fix)
- One pad per liquid. A compound pad never touches polish.

### 8.3 Running the machine
- Pad flat ON the paint before you pull the trigger. Off while still on the paint, then lift. `[HOUSE]`
- Spread on speed 1 to 2 across the whole square. `[HOUSE]`
- Work on speed 4 (HF range for polishing is 4 to 6). `[HOUSE]` `[SOURCED 2026-10-07]`
- About 1 inch per second. Slower than feels right. `[HOUSE]`
- Crosshatch: left to right overlapping by half, then top to bottom. That is one set. 4 to 6 sets per square. `[HOUSE]`
- About 10 lb of pressure (practice on a bathroom scale). Last set: let the machine float on its own weight. `[HOUSE]`
- Rotation check: draw a marker line on the pad edge. If the line stops turning, ease up and flatten the pad. `[HOUSE from booklet]`
- Watch the polish: creamy, then see-through, then almost gone. When it goes clear, it is spent. Stop. `[HOUSE]`
- **Wipe once, at the end of the square's sets. Do not wipe and re-apply between passes.** (Oct 7 fix) `[HOUSE]`
- Then IPA mist, wipe with a clean towel, check with the light low and from a few feet back. `[HOUSE]`
- Curves: use the middle of the pad. Never tilt onto an edge. `[HOUSE]`
- Plastic bumpers: heat fast. Lighter pressure, lower speed, shorter time. `[HOUSE]`
- Too hot to keep your hand on: stop and let it cool. `[HOUSE]`
- Never park the machine spinning in one spot. `[HOUSE]`

### 8.4 Pad care during the job `[HOUSE]`
- Blow out or brush the pad after every square. A loaded pad stops cutting and starts smearing.
- Swap pads when loaded or hot, roughly every two panels, and at minimum a fresh yellow at the halfway point of the car.

### 8.5 Test spot and the ladder `[HOUSE]`
Tape a 2 x 2 ft square on the worst panel (the hood on Lando's Model Y). Split it with tape so you can compare halves.
1. Yellow + M210. Wipe, IPA, light. Swirls gone? That is the combo for the whole car (one-step).
2. Still swirled: maroon + M210 on the same square.
3. Still swirled and the panel reads 100 µm or more: maroon + Ultimate Compound (3 to 4 sets), wipe with its own towel, then **always** follow with yellow + M210 to clear the compound haze. Every compounded square gets both passes.
4. Still there after compound + polish: it is too deep. Note it, show the customer, do not chase it.
Rules: one compound pass per section, max. Show the customer both halves and re-quote before going further than what was sold. Write the winning combo on the job sheet.

### 8.6 Turtle Wax Polishing Compound: rejected `[HOUSE]`
On the never-on-paint list. The crusted cap means dried abrasive.

### 8.7 Time per section
About 4 minutes per 2 x 2 ft section on a one-step, including wipe and check. `[HOUSE from booklet time map]`

## 9. Protection and finishing

### 9.1 Meguiar's Hybrid Ceramic Wax (spray) `[SOURCED 2026-10-06]` `[VERIFY reconfirm label]`
- Standard method: on a WET car, spray, rinse, dry. No haze, no buffing, no waiting.
- "Wax as you dry" (used for Lando's first application): wet panel, light mist, dry with a clean towel, skip the second rinse. No cure time.
- Streaks or high spots: 2 to 3 sprays on a clean damp towel and re-wipe.
- A whole-car coat the next day counts as the second coat on a freshly polished panel.
- Protect the same day you polish: fresh polished paint is bare. `[HOUSE]`
- Superseded: "thin coat, let it haze 3 to 5 minutes, wipe" (booklet page 8). That is for paste or liquid waxes, not this spray. `[SUPERSEDED]`

### 9.2 Tires and trim `[HOUSE]`
- Tire must be completely dry. Spray onto the applicator, not the tire. Thin, even coat. Wipe the rubber-to-wheel edge. Let it set a few minutes before the car moves.
- Thick coat slings black spots down the side on the first drive.
- Grey faded trim needs a trim restorer, not tire dressing.

### 9.3 Glass `[HOUSE]`
- Two towels: one wet, one dry. Never a towel that touched wax or polish.
- Inside: wipe side to side. Outside: up and down. A streak then tells you which side it's on.
- Drop windows 1 to 2 inches and clean the top edge.
- Windshield from the passenger seat for the far corner.
- Glass is last: last thing inside, last thing outside.

### 9.4 Headlights `[HOUSE]`
Blue Magic Headlight Lens Restorer by hand, about two minutes per light, wipe clean. It brightens but does not hold: without a sealant on top, they yellow again in about a month. Sell it as a refresh ($30/pair), not a fix.

## 10. Interior SOP `[HOUSE from booklet and Panel Map]`
Order: everything out (trash, mats, car seats, loose items) then mats (hose, brush, rinse, stand in the sun) then knock it down from the top (headliner, vents, seams, seat rails) then vacuum top down (seats, under seats, carpet, trunk; slide seats forward, then back) then wipe hard surfaces top down then spots then dress plastics matte then glass last.

Rules:
- Spray the towel, never the panel. Overspray lands on glass and screens.
- Nothing shiny on the dash. It glares in the windshield. If it looks wet, you used too much.
- Screens: dry microfiber or a screen-safe cleaner only.
- Headliner: barely damp, blot only. Soak it and the glue lets go.
- Steering wheel and pedals: clean, dry, zero slick.
- Pet hair: rubber tool (Analan) or pumice first, then vacuum. Three passes minimum. Pet hair is quoted separately.
- Cup holders and seams: Dremel Versa bristle pad, then vacuum.
- Leather: cleaner, then conditioner. `[VERIFY products owned]`
- Wet carpet needs air before handoff. Crack the windows or run the fan.
- Personal items go in one bag the customer can see. Money or valuables go on the dash, and you say something.
- Times: clean daily driver about 1 hr 5 min. Family car with pet hair, crumbs, stains: 2 hrs or more. Price interiors by condition and look in the back seat before you quote.

## 12. Motorcycles `[HOUSE]`

### 12.1 Bike rules
- **No tire shine on a motorcycle. Ever.** Not the sidewall, not near it.
- Nothing slick on brake rotors, grips, pegs, or the seat.
- Bike must be stone cold. Water on hot pipes stains chrome; bug remover on a hot exhaust bakes on.
- Hose on low. No pressure at the chain, wheel bearings, electrics, under the seat, or the air intake.
- Dry with the air compressor, every crevice.
- Matte (including Harley "Denim") paint: no compound, polish, or wax. Matte-safe cleaner only.
- Windshields are plastic: no alcohol, no glass cleaner, no compound, no clay. `[VERIFY approved plastic cleaner]`
- Hand-polish tanks and fenders. The 6 in. pad does not fit a tank. A 3 in. machine comes later from bike revenue.
- Chrome: chrome polish, then a coat of ceramic wax.
- Chain: chain-specific cleaner and chain lube only. Household degreaser strips the O-rings.
- Tell every rider: brakes are wet, first stop is soft, ride easy for a block.

## 13. Cleanup and laundering `[HOUSE]`

### 13.1 Pads (same night, before polish dries in)
Pull the pad off the backing plate. Warm water (not hot; hot is hard on foam) with a drop of laundry detergent. Work the face until the water runs clear. Press the water out, never wring. Dry face down on a rack. Fully dry before the next use. Dried compound in a pad is a dead pad. Wipe polish off the backing plate.

### 13.2 Towels: three piles that never mix
1. Water-only (wash and drying towels).

## 14. Backing plate (researched Oct 7) `[SOURCED 2026-10-07]`
- The Bauer 56367 plate is 6 in., bolt-on, M8 x 1.25. **The $6.99 BAUER 6 in. backing pad on the HF shelf (item 64913) is 5/16 in.-24 thread. It does not fit the 56367.**
- Before buying, check (unplugged): center bolt tight; pad centered; hooks not bald or melted; plate not cracked or warped (spin it by hand and watch the edge).
- Replace with a **6 in. (150 mm) plate made for a long-throw, mounted with an M8 bolt.** Do not downsize to stop vibration: long-throw machines are counterweighted for 5 or 6 in. plates, and a 3 in. plate causes violent vibration and can wear out the bearings.
- First call: HF tech/parts line 1-888-866-5797, ask for the replacement backing pad for item 56367. `[VERIFY availability]`

## 21. Fix-It seeds (symptom first, for the troubleshooting engine)
Format: SAY IT (search words) / STOP IF / 30-SECOND CHECKS / LIKELY CAUSES (most likely first) / DO THIS / RELATED.

**FIX-01 Polisher jerks, walks, or hops side to side**
- Say it: jerking, hopping, walking, bouncing, pulling, fighting me, wants to go left and right
- Stop if: grinding noise, burning smell, visible plate wobble, abnormal vibration
- Checks (unplugged): center bolt tight; pad centered on the plate; hooks not bald or melted; plate not cracked (spin it)
- Causes: pad tilted onto an edge; pushing instead of guiding (grip too tight, pressure over ~10 lb); polish uneven, too much, or too little; pad off-center or loose plate; speed too low for a 20 mm throw; curved panel with a flat pad
- Do this: re-center the pad, spread at speed 1 to 2, work at 4, two hands, loose grip, guide don't push, keep it flat, watch the marker line keep turning
- Note: a 20 mm long-throw moves more than a short-throw; some float is normal `[VERIFY wording]`
- Related: FIX-02, FIX-03, FIX-23, Level 4 machine control

**FIX-02 Pad stops spinning (marker line slows or stops)**
- Causes: too much pressure, tilted pad, curve, too much product
- Do this: ease pressure, flatten, speed up one notch, check product amount

**FIX-03 Heavy vibration**
- Stop if: abnormal vibration (manual says stop)
- Causes: loose or worn backing plate, off-center pad, uneven compound, damaged pad
- Do this: unplug, run the FIX-01 checks; center the pad; spread product evenly; replace the plate (FIX-23)

**FIX-04 Polish looks dry, can't finish the section**
- Causes: pad not primed, pad dried out, section too big, panel hot or in sun
- Do this: mist the pad face with water; 1 to 2 drops; keep sections 2 x 2 ft; shade and cool panel

**FIX-05 Using too much polish and too much time**
- Cause: wiping and re-applying between passes; full dose every section
- Do this: prime once; 1 to 2 drops per section after that; 4 to 6 sets, wipe once at the end

**FIX-06 Slinging or splatter**
- Causes: too much product; started or stopped off the paint; spreading at high speed
- Do this: pad on paint before trigger, off before lift, spread at 1 to 2

**FIX-07 Pad flies off or won't stick**
- Causes: worn hooks on the plate or pad, heat
- Do this: inspect hooks; replace the pad or plate; let it cool

**FIX-08 Polish is dusting**
- Causes: worked too long, dry pad, too much heat
- Do this: shorter cycles, mist the pad, less product, stop when it goes clear

**FIX-09 Paint color on the pad: STOP**
- You are through the clear (unless it's old single-stage paint). Stop, photograph, tell the customer, log it. Do not touch that panel again.

**FIX-10 Swirls still there after polishing**
- Checks: did you IPA wipe? (polish oils hide swirls); fingernail test
- Do this: next rung of the ladder (8.5); if it survives compound + polish, it stays

**FIX-11 Haze after compound**
- Do this: every compounded square gets the yellow + M210 pass. Never ship a compounded panel without it

**FIX-12 Panel too hot**
- Do this: stop, let it cool; lighter pressure, lower speed on plastic bumpers; work in shade

**FIX-13 White residue on trim or badges**
- Cause: untaped trim
- Do this: tape before polishing; remove residue with a soft brush and a trim-safe cleaner `[VERIFY method]`

**FIX-14 Dirt still on the paint after washing**
- Causes: caked buildup, short pre-rinse, no dwell, bonded contamination
- Do this: longer pre-rinse; pre-soak with dwell (5.3); Bug & Tar on bugs and tar; clay what's bonded; never scrub harder

**FIX-15 Water spots after drying**
- Do this: prevention list (5.4); fresh spots: damp towel + ceramic spray; mineral spots: water-spot remover `[VERIFY]`; etched: polish

**FIX-16 Clay grabbing or leaving marks**
- Do this: more lube, lighter pressure, knead to a clean face; marring from clay gets polished out

**FIX-17 Dropped clay or mitt**
- Bar: trash it. Mitt: rinse thoroughly per maker `[VERIFY]`. Never put either back on paint unchecked

**FIX-18 Ceramic wax streaks or high spots**
- Do this: 2 to 3 sprays on a clean damp towel, re-wipe; shade, cool panel

**FIX-19 Glass streaks**
- Causes: contaminated towel (wax or polish), one-towel wipe
- Do this: two clean glass towels; inside side to side, outside up and down

**FIX-20 Tire shine slinging**
- Do this: apply to the applicator, thin coat, wipe the edge, let it set before the car moves

**FIX-21 Gauge reading looks wrong**
- Checks: zeroed on the plate, not the car? Held flat? In SNG mode? Fe/NFe auto-detected?
- Note: 20 µm swings within a foot are normal

**FIX-22 Reading under 90, under 75, or way above the rest**
- Do this: use the bands table (7.3). Tell the customer before the pad touches paint

**FIX-23 Backing plate damaged or needs replacing**
- Do this: section 14. Do NOT buy HF item 64913 for the 56367

**FIX-24 Setup is taking forever**
- Do this: kit staging (18.3), pre-stage the night before, setup timer

**FIX-25 Running out of time**
- Do this: check the time map (17); don't start polishing after the cutoff; split interior to another day on personal cars; for customers, quote the real hours

**FIX-26 Tesla windows dropped, wipers ran, or Sentry fired during the wash**
- Do this: Car Wash Mode before water (section 11)

**FIX-27 Customer asks "can you get this scratch out?"**
- Do this: fingernail test with them; read the script (16); test spot; never promise

**FIX-28 Found existing damage, or you broke something**
- Do this: stop, photograph, tell them now, note it on the job sheet; offer the fix in the same sentence

**FIX-29 Customer pushes back on price**
- Do this: floor price (15.3); offer a smaller package, never a smaller price for the same work

**FIX-30 Motorcycle: what can't I use?**
- Do this: bike rules (12.1). No tire shine, nothing slick on rotors/grips/pegs/seat, no alcohol on the windshield

**FIX-31 Pet hair won't come out**
- Do this: rubber tool first, then vacuum, three passes, quote it as an add-on

**FIX-32 Dash looks greasy or shiny**
- Do this: too much product; wipe with a clean damp towel, then dry; spray the towel, not the dash

**FIX-33 Can I polish today? (cold, hot, wind)**
- Do this: shade, no wind, hand-on-panel test; below about 50 F, garage it or move the date `[VERIFY]`
