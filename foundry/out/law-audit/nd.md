# North Dakota (ND) law audit

Checked 2026-10-27. Status: done. Ledger: `cloudflare/data/law/nd.json`.

Official code access: North Dakota Century Code chapter 57-28 fetched as a PDF from ndlegis.gov (Legislative Council). Chapter 57-24 (sale of land for delinquent taxes) is repealed (S.L. 1999, ch. 503, § 47).

## Finding

North Dakota does not sell tax liens or tax lien certificates. Chapter 57-24 (sale of land for delinquent taxes) was
repealed in 1999. Under N.D.C.C. ch. 57-28 the county auditor gives notice of foreclosure once taxes are two or more years
past due, the lien is foreclosed on October 1 after notice, and the auditor issues a tax deed to the county. The county
appraises the land and sells it at an annual auction beginning the third Tuesday of November to the highest qualified
bidder (cash, or one-fourth down on a contract for deed at no more than 12% interest). Unsold parcels may be sold
privately before the next annual sale. Excess proceeds go to the record owner who files an undisputed claim within a
90-day retention period (§ 57-28-20). The site's "December auctions", "3-year redemption" and "12% lien rate" are not in
the statute and were corrected.

## 2023 to 2026 enacted-law search

The fetched chapter PDF (ndlegis.gov/cencode/t57c28.pdf) does not print section histories, so enacted 2023 to 2025
session laws could not be identified from it. The excess-proceeds text in § 57-28-20 (90-day retention, claim by record
owner) reflects the current code. The Legislative Branch's session-law search for the 2023 and 2025 sessions was not run
this session for time; recheck in a later run.
