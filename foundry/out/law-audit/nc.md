# North Carolina (NC) law audit

Checked 2026-11-01. Status: done. Ledger: `cloudflare/data/law/nc.json`.

Official code access: North Carolina General Statutes Chapter 105 Article 26 (sections 105-349 to 105-378, including 105-374, 105-375, 105-376) and G.S. 1-339.64, from ncleg.gov (North Carolina General Assembly). Session Law 2024-44 (S.B. 802) was read; it changes §105-375(i), §105-374(k) and §105-376(b) only to add C-PACE assessments as exceptions, effective July 1, 2024.

Finding: the site called North Carolina a "tax deed" state with "no redemption", a "unique" 5% upset-bid window that lets "any party" win the property, year-round sales, a 5-10% deposit, strong tenant protections, IRS liens that survive, and best inventory in Mecklenburg, Wake and Durham. The General Statutes say: a tax lien is foreclosed either by a court action like a mortgage foreclosure (§105-374) or by an in rem method (§105-375); the §105-374 sale is a public auction to the highest bidder at the courthouse door; an increased bid may be filed within 10 days after the report of sale; an upset bid must top the last bid by 5% and at least $750 (G.S. 1-339.64); the commissioner may require a deposit of up to 20%; redemption before confirmation needs taxes, penalties, interest and costs. Opinion, inventory, tenant and IRS claims have no source and were removed. The "no OTC" claim is now "not set by statute, confirm with the county".
