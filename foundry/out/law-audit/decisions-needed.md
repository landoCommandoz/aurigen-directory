## Decision A: 11 claims say "not available" or "varies" with no official source behind them

The statutes read say nothing on these points. The old text stated them as fact. The audit kept the wording but flagged it. Choose one for the group:
- **Soften (recommended):** replace each with "Confirm with the county or municipality" so nothing is stated that no official source supports.
- **Keep as written:** the site keeps saying "Not generally available" or "Set by each borough or city" until the gap queue reads the local rules.

1. Alaska, bid method: "Set by each borough or city". AS 29.45.460 sets no method; city and borough ordinances were not read.
2. Alaska, deposit: "Varies by borough or city" (English and Spanish, 2 claims). Same reason.
3. Alaska, over-the-counter: "Not generally available" and "No statewide OTC program" (3 claims: the availability field, plus the note in English and in Spanish). No state rule, but municipalities may sell unsold property by ordinance.
4. California, over-the-counter: "Not generally available". No official text read; the State Controller manual says unsold property may be re-offered at a lower minimum bid.
5. Hawaii, over-the-counter: "Not generally available". No official source read for any county.
6. Louisiana, over-the-counter: "Not generally available". Unsold liens go to the local government; no section read on buying them later.
7. Maine, over-the-counter: "Not generally available". The law requires a broker listing of town-held property, which may be a form of over-the-counter sale.
8. New Mexico, over-the-counter: "Not generally available". The Taxation and Revenue Department pages do not mention it either way.

(Alaska is 6 claims and the other five states 1 each, so 11 in all.)
