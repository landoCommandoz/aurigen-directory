# Kansas (KS) law audit

Checked 2026-11-01. Status: done. Ledger: `cloudflare/data/law/ks.json`.

Official code access: Kansas Statutes Annotated 79-2004, 79-2401a, 79-2801, 79-2803, 79-2804, 79-2804b and 79-2805, current text from ksrevisor.gov (Kansas Office of the Revisor of Statutes).

Finding: the site described Kansas as a tax lien state where investors buy liens and earn 12% or "up to 15%". The statutes read describe something else: the county bids in property at the delinquent tax sale, holds it (redemption 2 years, or 3 years for homestead and other property, §79-2401a), then brings a district court foreclosure action (§79-2801); the sheriff sells at public auction for the highest and best bid, the county's own bid is capped, and the sheriff executes the deed after the court confirms the sale (§79-2804). Unpaid taxes accrue the §79-2968 rate plus five percentage points a year (§79-2004). No 12% or 15% figure and no investor lien certificate appears in the sections read. Whether any private lien sale exists elsewhere in chapter 79 was not searched (gap KS-1), so the site now says "no private lien certificates are described in these sections".

Pass 2 trim: redemption shows only the 2-year period its quote states, with "Other periods apply: Confirm with the county"; the 3-year period has its own claim. Lando decision A applied to dates, place, registration, payment, over-the-counter sales. Removed with no source: Sedgwick/Johnson advice, rural land claims, partition actions, road access.
