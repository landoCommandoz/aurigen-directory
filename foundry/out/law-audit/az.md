# Arizona (AZ) law audit

Checked 2026-11-01. Status: done. Ledger: `cloudflare/data/law/az.json`.

Official code access: Arizona Revised Statutes title 42, chapter 18 (sections 42-18053, 42-18101 to 42-18127, 42-18151 to 42-18154, 42-18201 to 42-18204), current text from azleg.gov (Arizona Legislature). Sections 42-18115 and 42-18204 show Laws 2026, ch. 66 and ch. 256 (effective until 10/1/28).

**Conflict resolved (AZ).** The first run's summary said lien holders must use judicial foreclosure, "not a treasurer's deed", and its freshness note said redemption runs "until treasurer deed delivered". The Arizona Revised Statutes show both are partly right. § 42-18201 requires an action in superior court to foreclose the right to redeem (naming the county treasurer), and § 42-18204(A)(1)(b) then has the court direct the county treasurer to execute and deliver a deed to the winning party. § 42-18152 lets the owner redeem at any time until that treasurer's deed is delivered. The site now says: foreclosure action in superior court, and the judgment directs the treasurer to deliver the deed. "Not a treasurer's deed" was removed.

Other findings: "reverse-bid ... 1% increments", "0% bids accepted", "Pima online Feb 2026", GovEase/RealAuction/aztaxsale.info, "1-2% reach foreclosure", county names, and the SB 1291 note have no support in the statute text and were removed or replaced by "Confirm with the county". The OTC route is real: § 42-18113 assigns no-bid liens to the state and § 42-18122 has the treasurer resell them to any person who pays the amount then due plus subsequent taxes ("year-round" is not stated).
